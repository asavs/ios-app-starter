# Settings sections: About, privacy and data, acknowledgements, reset

Checked 2026-10-30 by claude-mac for board tasks ios-6dd.1 (this research) and ios-6dd.2 (the implementation). Built as: pending.

Why: two of the five apps surveyed for module round two (epic ios-6dd) wrote the same four things by hand in their Settings: an About section (version and build, a line on what the app does with data, a footer saying text size, contrast and Reduce Motion follow the iPhone settings), a privacy and local data screen (works offline, what is stored, how to delete it), an acknowledgements screen (third-party art and code), and a reset or delete local data action. The template's Settings module shows only the version. Grades: A, Apple documentation; B, a named practitioner or a maintained open-source project; C, unattributed.

## What Apple requires

- **A privacy policy link inside the app.** App Review Guideline 5.1.1(i): "All apps must include a link to their privacy policy in the App Store Connect metadata field and within the app in an easily accessible manner." The policy must say what data the app collects and how, name third parties with access, and "explain its data retention/deletion policies and describe how a user can revoke consent and/or request deletion of the user's data." (A) https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#data-collection-and-storage
- **App Privacy details on the product page** are mandatory for every submission, with a required, publicly reachable privacy policy URL. Data "processed only on device is not 'collected' and does not need to be disclosed", so a local-only app declares no collected data but still needs the policy URL. (A) https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/
- **Account deletion is required only when the app creates accounts** (5.1.1(v); Apple's support page: "Apps submitted to the App Store that support account creation must also let people initiate deletion of their account within the app", "typically... in the app's account settings"). An app without accounts has no deletion obligation; the reset action below is good practice, not a rule. It becomes the place account deletion plugs in when the sign-in module (ios-s5.1) arrives. (A) https://developer.apple.com/support/offering-account-deletion-in-your-app
- **A privacy manifest for required-reason APIs.** `UserDefaults` is a required-reason API; an app that reads or writes its own defaults declares `NSPrivacyAccessedAPICategoryUserDefaults` with reason `CA92.1` ("information that is only accessible to the app itself") in `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy`. "Starting May 1, 2024, apps that don't describe their use of required reason API in their privacy manifest file aren't accepted by App Store Connect." A Swift package target declares its own file and must list it under `resources:`. (A) https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/describing-use-of-required-reason-api and https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/adding-a-privacy-manifest-to-your-app-or-third-party-sdk
  - Finding: this repository has no `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` anywhere, while the app and four modules (Settings, Onboarding, TabShell, Cues) use `UserDefaults`. TestFlight uploads have gone through, so enforcement for first-party code is evidently a warning email rather than a rejection today, but the rule is written as a rejection. Add the manifests with the implementation; it is one small file per target.

## What Apple says about the screen

- **HIG Settings:** "Put general, infrequently changed settings in your custom settings area"; "Minimize the number of settings you offer"; "Respect people's systemwide settings and avoid including redundant versions of them". The page says nothing about version, legal or privacy rows: they are a convention, not a rule. The convention is still worth following because reviewers and users look for them. (A) https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/settings
- **HIG Privacy:** "Be transparent about how your app collects and uses people's data"; the product page's privacy details are where Apple places that transparency. Nothing on an in-app privacy screen beyond the pre-permission screen. (A) https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy
- **Confirming a reset.** HIG Alerts: "when people take an uncommon destructive action that they can't undo, it's important to display an alert in case they initiated the action accidentally"; "A specific button title like 'Erase,' 'Convert,' 'Clear,' or 'Delete' helps people understand the action"; "Always use 'Cancel'"; "Use the destructive style to identify a button that performs a destructive action people didn't deliberately choose", so Empty Trash in Finder is not red because the person asked for it. HIG Action sheets: "Use an action sheet, not an alert, to offer choices related to an intentional action"; "A SwiftUI confirmation dialog includes a Cancel button by default"; "Provide a message only if necessary". For a reset the person tapped a button for, a `confirmationDialog` with one destructive-role button named for what it erases, plus the automatic Cancel, is the fit. (A) https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/alerts and https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/action-sheets
- **Version strings.** `CFBundleShortVersionString` is "a user-visible string for the version of the bundle", "three period-separated integers, such as 10.14.1"; `CFBundleVersion` "identifies an iteration of the bundle". The Settings module already formats them as `1.0 (42)` (`AppInfo.version`). (A) https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/information-property-list/cfbundleshortversionstring

## What apps do

- **NetNewsWire** (MIT, iOS/Settings/AboutView.swift): a copyable "Version x (build y)" line, the authors, a website link, then Credits, Thanks, Dedication sections and a copyright line, under the title "About NetNewsWire". (B) https://github.com/Ranchero-Software/NetNewsWire
- **Ice Cubes** (Apache-2.0, App/Tabs/Settings/AboutView.swift): website and source links, the version with a copyright line, a Telemetry section linking the analytics vendor's privacy page, and a "built with" list of packages as links. Its settings tab groups General, Other and App sections with `Label` rows behind `NavigationLink`s. (B) https://github.com/Dimillian/IceCubesApp
- **The surveyed private apps** (patterns only): About This Build with `LabeledContent` rows ("Version", "Your data: stays on this iPhone") and an accessibility footer; a Privacy notice screen with Works Offline, Local Data and Controls sections; an Acknowledgements screen of `Link`s with licence notes; a "reset local data" action on a recovery screen and a "delete local activity history" action in Settings.
- **Acknowledgement generators.** AcknowList (MIT; iOS 13+, SwiftUI view `AcknowListSwiftUIView`) reads `Package.resolved` or a CocoaPods plist and can fetch missing licences from the GitHub API at runtime; AckGen and fastlane-plugin-acknowledgements generate a `Settings.bundle` pane so licences appear in the system Settings app, the CocoaPods convention. Diligence (inseven) offers an About screen with actions, credits and licences for iOS and macOS. (B) https://github.com/vtourraine/AcknowList, https://github.com/MartinP7r/AckGen, https://github.com/SimonRice/fastlane-plugin-acknowledgements, https://github.com/inseven/diligence
  - The template should not fetch licences at runtime (a network call from a privacy screen, and GitHub rate limits) and should not depend on a `Settings.bundle` (HIG: "Add only the most rarely changed options to the system-provided Settings app"). A hand-kept list the app fills in is enough: the template itself has no third-party code, and an app adds entries when it adopts a dependency. Generation from `Package.resolved` can come later as a script, not a runtime feature.

## Recommendation

Extend the Settings module rather than adding a module: the About section already lives there, every app that has Settings wants these rows, and an app without Settings has nowhere to put them anyway.

- **About section** keeps the version row (make its value selectable so people can copy it into a bug report, as NetNewsWire does) and gains rows the app supplies through an `about:` builder: a Privacy and Data link and an Acknowledgements link, plus any app-specific `LabeledContent`. An optional footer sentence states that text size, contrast and Reduce Motion follow the system; the HIG says not to duplicate those settings, so the footer is the template's way of saying "we didn't".
- **Privacy and Data screen** built from a `PrivacyNotice` value: where data lives (on this iPhone, iCloud, the app's servers), what is stored, what leaves the device, the privacy policy `URL` (the 5.1.1(i) link, shown as a `Link` row), and how to delete data (pointing at the reset action). The template's notice says everything stays on the phone; an app rewrites the sentences.
- **Acknowledgements screen** built from `[Acknowledgement]` (name, author, licence, URL). Optional: when the array is empty the link is hidden.
- **Reset local data**: a destructive-role button in its own section at the end of the form, with a `confirmationDialog` titled by what it erases and a single destructive "Erase" button. The module provides `LocalDataReset`, a list of erasers an app composes: `.defaults(suiteName)` (`removePersistentDomain(forName:)`), `.keychain(service)` (the module's `KeychainStore`), and `.custom { }` for the database store (`ModelContainer.erase()` on iOS 18 and later, file deletion on iOS 17; ios-s8.1 adds it) and module records such as onboarding. Nothing restarts the app; views re-read their stores.
- **Privacy manifests**: `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` with `CA92.1` for the app target (in `project.yml`) and for each module target that touches `UserDefaults`, declared in `Package.swift` resources. `scripts/check-modules.py` can warn when a module target uses `UserDefaults` without one.
- **Human step**: every app needs a public privacy policy URL before submission (App Store Connect and 5.1.1(i)). Record it in `docs/human-steps.md`; the template leaves the URL `nil` and the screen hides the row until it is set.

## Open questions

- Whether Apple enforces missing first-party required-reason declarations as a rejection today or only as an email: the written rule says rejection from 2024-05-01; the template uploaded without a manifest in 2026-10. Adding the manifest is right either way.
- A licence generator from `Package.resolved` (what AcknowList reads) as a script in `scripts/`, once an app depends on third-party code.
- Account deletion lands with the sign-in module (ios-s5.1) and must satisfy the support page's rules (whole account, in-app, no support flow), not this reset.
