# Background audio cues

Checked 2026-10-27. A: Apple policy, documentation or DTS; B: developer report. Recommendation: propose an optional cue module with separate foreground and scheduled-notification adapters. Do not make silent audio a starter default.

Apple permits background services only for intended purposes under [review guideline 2.5.4, A](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). A silent loop used solely to keep a timer process alive risks rejection; the guideline does not explicitly decide every silent-buffer case, and review outcome cannot be predicted. Apple DTS says apps are typically suspended in the background, recommends scheduled local notifications for a timer and warns of background-mode scrutiny ([DTS, A](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/132078)). Debugger attachment changes suspension behavior, so test from the Home Screen on a locked phone without Xcode attached. No claim here establishes that the current Val Time implementation was rejected or tested.

Schedule local notifications in advance for meaningful phase events when permission is granted, reconcile and cancel them on pause/edit/end, and handle denied authorization. Notifications are system-delivered alerts, not a general way to run speech or code on schedule. They have user-facing presentation and scheduling limits, so test long sessions and notification settings rather than promising every 3-second cue. `BGTaskScheduler` is system-scheduled background work, not a precise timer ([Apple, A](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/backgroundtasks)). A Live Activity displays session state but is not a continuous execution grant ([ActivityKit, A](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit)). AlarmKit supports prominent user-authorized alarms and countdowns on iOS 26+, overriding Focus and silent mode; it is not a drop-in quiet cue mechanism ([WWDC25, A](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/230/)). Offer an iOS 17 notification fallback.

For legitimate foreground audio, AVAudioEngine can schedule generated PCM buffers; configure the audio session deliberately. `.mixWithOthers` enables mixing, while ducking is a separate choice. The playback category affects the silent switch and lock-screen behavior, and background mode should be used for genuine playback, not assumed timing entitlement ([audio session, A](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession), [mix option, A](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession/categoryoptions-swift.struct/mixwithothers)). Activate when needed, handle interruption, route changes and media-services reset, and test music, Bluetooth, lock, denial, and interruptions on device. Preserve user expectations and do not silently claim guaranteed background speech.

Apple's archived `UILocalNotification` documentation describes a 64-pending limit for that deprecated API ([Apple, A](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uilocalnotification)); it does not establish the current `UNUserNotificationCenter` limit. Measure actual behavior and handle scheduling errors on supported runtimes. Apple also documents checking notification authorization and settings before scheduling ([Apple, A](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/asking-permission-to-use-notifications)).

## Fifteen-minute routine

A regular 15-minute session has 60 interval boundaries at 15 seconds, 30 at 30 seconds or 15 at 60 seconds, counting the final boundary. Three extra one-shot countdown notifications per boundary would raise those counts to 240, 120 or 60; whether a particular routine needs all three should be calculated from its actual schedule. Existing pending requests elsewhere in the app consume the same budget. Schedule a bounded set in chronological order, use stable IDs, fetch pending requests and surface errors. Reconcile on pause, edits, cancellation and foreground return; a suspended app cannot refill the queue on demand.

Apple's old `UILocalNotification` documentation specifies 64 soonest requests, but is deprecated. The maintained [flutter_local_notifications README](https://github.com/MaikuB/flutter_local_notifications/blob/master/flutter_local_notifications/README.md#ios-pending-notifications-limit) reports 64 and says modern iOS retains the last set, not necessarily the soonest (B). Its [issue 2312](https://github.com/MaikuB/flutter_local_notifications/issues/2312) reports another failure above 64 and is unresolved as a general platform diagnosis (C). No current Apple contract or local iOS 17/18/26 measurement was found: treat 64 as a conservative planning assumption, not a guaranteed ordering or exact current limit. Test 63, 64 and 65 requests, inspect pending and delivered requests, repeat on physical devices without a debugger, and budget for other features. Notification authorization, sound settings, Focus and user choices still affect delivery.

Named notification sounds must be in supported formats and shorter than 30 seconds; longer files play the default sound ([UNNotificationSound, A](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/unnotificationsound)). A prerecorded phrase can represent a known cue, but arbitrary synthesized phase names cannot be generated by a suspended process at delivery. Active notifications may play a sound; time-sensitive ones can break through notification controls, but can be disabled by the person and should be reserved for genuinely time-sensitive events, not automatically applied to every frequent cue ([interruption levels, A](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/unnotificationinterruptionlevel), [WWDC21, A](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10091/)). Critical alerts are a distinct entitlement and are not a routine timer fallback.

AlarmKit on iOS 26+ accepts custom sounds, but Apple describes prominent alarms that override silent mode and Focus when necessary ([WWDC25, A](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/230/), [sample, A](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/alarmkit/scheduling-an-alarm-with-alarmkit)). A custom quiet file does not establish a supported quiet, non-disruptive repeating interval-cue contract. Authorization, presentation, interruption, overlapping alarms and physical-device behavior need tests; do not schedule 60 prominent alarms as if they were discreet haptics. AlarmKit also cannot cover iOS 17.

| Option | What it preserves | What changes or remains uncertain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foreground cues plus pre-scheduled local notifications | System delivery of bounded phase alerts while suspended; works on iOS 17 | Permission and user settings apply; alerts have notification presentation; dynamic speech, haptics, three-second sequences and dense sessions may not fit. No claim of exact delivery. |
| Genuine continuous user-selected audio program with cues | Can fit an audio-centered guided experience and support background playback | Changes the product experience and power use; interruptions and review remain; silence solely to retain runtime is not established as sanctioned. |
| AlarmKit on iOS 26+ | Prominent authorized countdown completion | Overrides quiet settings and is not a subtle repeating cue; separate iOS 17 fallback needed. |
| Silent keep-alive | Developer reports show it can run cues | No guaranteed execution or review acceptance; don't make it default or describe it as approved. |

Recommend the first option as the starter's conservative default, with a clear capability result before a session starts: indicate whether all planned cues fit and whether sounds are authorized. Let the host choose a reduced cue plan or keep the screen active; never silently promise all cues. If continuous guided audio is a genuine user-selected product feature, prototype it separately and seek actual App Review feedback. This recommendation does not claim Apple has approved a precise 15-minute dense-cue design.

## Proposed boundary

`CueEvent(id, scheduledInstant, kind, optionalText)` is an immutable host-defined event; `CuePolicy(sound, countdown, voice, haptics)` controls outputs. A `CueCoordinator` accepts the policy and injected `ForegroundCueOutput`, `NotificationScheduler`, and clock; methods prepare a bounded plan, report authorization/capacity, cancel by session ID, and handle a foreground event. A `CueSchedulingResult` reports unsupported, denied, partial or scheduled rather than promising delivery. The host owns wording, notification permission timing and phase semantics. The Settings section edits policy through injected bindings; Theme styles the section without the core importing Theme. SessionClock emits events and does not depend on audio. Tests use recording fakes; demo shows cue toggles, a short phase and a scheduled/denied state. Screen-awake is a separate coordinator because it is global lifecycle state, not a cue output. Exact Swift signatures and actor isolation require implementation review.
